\begin{abstract}
A \emph{matching cut} of a graph is an edge cut that is a matching.
Lucke, Martin, Paulusma and Siggers
[arXiv:2501.08735] determined the complexity of \textsc{Matching Cut}
on bipartite graphs of every radius and diameter except radius
exactly~3, which they posed as an open problem. We resolve this case:
\textsc{Matching Cut} is NP-complete on bipartite graphs of radius~3.
The reduction is from a signed variant of \textsc{Not-1-in-3-Sat},
built on a BFS-layer analysis of radius-3 bipartite centers and a
guard gadget battery that eliminates every matching cut not aligned
with the intended encoding. The combinatorial core of the proof (the
construction, its structural properties, and the equivalence in both
directions) is machine-verified in Lean~4 with zero holes.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A \emph{matching cut} of a graph $G$ is a partition of $V(G)$ into two
nonempty classes such that the edges joining the classes form a
matching. Equivalently, in the colouring form used throughout this
paper: a red/blue colouring of $V(G)$ using both colours in which
every vertex has at most one neighbour of the opposite colour. The
\textsc{Matching Cut} decision problem, introduced in the context of
networks immune to single-link failures, has a long dichotomy
literature over graph classes.

Lucke, Martin, Paulusma and Siggers~\cite{LMPS25} determined the
complexity of \textsc{Matching Cut} (and several variants) on
bipartite graphs of bounded radius and diameter: polynomial for radius
at most~2 and for diameter at most~3, NP-complete for radius at
least~4 and diameter at least~4, leaving exactly one cell open, posed
as their Open Problem~1: bipartite graphs of radius exactly~3. We
close it.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
\textsc{Matching Cut} is NP-complete on bipartite graphs of radius
exactly~3.
\end{theorem}

Membership in \NP{} is trivial. Hardness is by reduction from a
signed variant of \textsc{Not-1-in-3-Sat} (Section~\ref{sec:source}),
via the construction $G(F)$ of Section~\ref{sec:construction}. The
proof decomposes into four independent parts: structural properties
of $G(F)$ (Section~\ref{sec:structure}); a case analysis showing every
matching cut of $G(F)$ must take a constrained form
(Section~\ref{sec:cases}); an exact reformulation of that constrained
form as a set problem (Section~\ref{sec:reform}); and the equivalence
of the set problem with satisfiability of $F$
(Section~\ref{sec:island}).

The technical heart is the interplay of two facts. First, a bipartite
graph of radius~3 with center $u$ has a rigid four-layer BFS structure
in which every vertex of the center's side sits within distance~2 of
$u$; this creates a per-vertex ``budget'' vocabulary in which matching
cuts can be completely described. Second, the known hardness
constructions for \textsc{Matching Cut} are inherently radius-4:
their gadget machinery requires vertices far from any single center.
The reduction squeezes clause machinery into the radius-3 ball by
routing all constraint logic through the third layer, and then
eliminates the spurious cuts this permissiveness creates with a
\emph{guard battery}: a single killer vertex whose neighbourhood
freezes under every possible deviation, making any cut that does not
follow the intended encoding immediately infeasible.

\section{Preliminaries and the source problem}\label{sec:source}

All graphs are finite, simple, and undirected. For a connected graph
$G$ and $u \in V(G)$, $\mathrm{ecc}(u) = \max_v \mathrm{dist}(u,v)$;
the radius of $G$ is $\min_u \mathrm{ecc}(u)$. A \emph{matching-cut
colouring} of $G$ is a function $c : V(G) \to \{\mathrm{R},
\mathrm{B}\}$ such that both colours are used and every vertex has at
most one neighbour of the opposite colour; $G$ has a matching cut iff
it has a matching-cut colouring.

\textsc{Signed Not-1-in-3-Sat}: variables $x_1,\dots,x_n$; each
clause is an ordered triple of literals (repetitions allowed within
and across clauses); a clause is satisfied iff the number of true
literals among its three positions is $\neq 1$. NP-completeness
follows from Schaefer's dichotomy: the constraint language of all
sign-patterns of $R(a,b,c) \equiv (a{+}b{+}c \neq 1)$ escapes all six
tractable classes (it is 0-valid and 1-valid, but its signed variants
exclude $000$ and $111$ respectively; it is not Horn, not dual-Horn
once signs are present, not affine ($|R|=5$), and not bijunctive).
We take the problem restricted to $n \ge 1$, $m \ge 1$; the
restriction is still NP-complete, since instances with $m=0$ or $n=0$
are always satisfiable and map to the fixed satisfiable instance
$(x_1, x_1, x_1)$.

\section{The construction}\label{sec:construction}

Fix a formula $F$ with $n \ge 1$ variables and $m \ge 1$ clauses. The
graph $G(F)$ has four layers.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{$L_0$:} the centre $u$.
\item \textbf{$L_1$ (witnesses), each adjacent to $u$:}
  shared variable witnesses $q_i$ ($i \in [n]$); the anchor witness
  $w_a$; a proxy witness $w_p$ for each clause position
  $p = (C,j)$, $j \in \{1,2,3\}$; a guard second-witness $w'_W$ for
  each $W \in \BW := \{q_1,\dots,q_n, w_a\} \cup \{w_p\}$; and four
  ballast witnesses $t^1_a, t^1_b, t^2_a, t^2_b$.
\item \textbf{$L_2$:} the anchor $a$; literal vertices $t_i, f_i$;
  proxy vertices $p_{C,j}$; a guard $g_W$ for each $W \in \BW$; and
  ballast vertices $T_1, T_2$.
\item \textbf{$L_3$:} gates $\gamma_i$; copies $c_{C,j,0}, c_{C,j,1}$;
  clause vertices $z_C$; and the killer $Z$.
\end{itemize}

Edges (and no others): $u$ is adjacent to every $L_1$ vertex.
$L_1$--$L_2$: $q_i \sim t_i, f_i, g_{q_i}$; \ $w_a \sim a, g_{w_a}$;
\ $w_p \sim p_{C,j}, g_{w_p}$ for $p=(C,j)$; \ $w'_W \sim g_W$; \
$t^1_a, t^1_b \sim T_1$ and $t^2_a, t^2_b \sim T_2$.
$L_2$--$L_3$: $\gamma_i \sim a, t_i, f_i$; \ $c_{C,j,k} \sim
\mathrm{base}(C,j)$ and $p_{C,j}$ for $k \in \{0,1\}$, where
$\mathrm{base}(C,j) = t_i$ if the $j$-th literal of $C$ is $x_i$ and
$f_i$ if it is $\lnot x_i$; \ $z_C \sim p_{C,1}, p_{C,2}, p_{C,3}$;
\ $Z \sim T_1, T_2$ and every guard $g_W$.

Thus $|V(G(F))| = 6n + 19m + 12$, and the construction is computable
in linear time. Three counts used repeatedly: $a$, each $t_i, f_i$,
and each $p_{C,j}$ have exactly one $L_1$-neighbour; each guard and
each ballast vertex has exactly two; every $L_3$ vertex has degree at
least two ($\gamma_i$: 3; copies: 2; $z_C$: 3; $Z$: $2 + |\BW| \ge
5$).

\section{Structural properties}\label{sec:structure}

\begin{claim}\label{cl:bip}
$G(F)$ is bipartite with parts $\{u\} \cup L_2$ and $L_1 \cup L_3$.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
Every edge listed joins the two parts: $u$--$L_1$, $L_1$--$L_2$, and
$L_2$--$L_3$; no edge within a part is present.
\end{proof}

\begin{claim}\label{cl:conn}
$G(F)$ is connected and $\mathrm{ecc}(u) = 3$; the labels $L_0,\dots,
L_3$ are the BFS layers from $u$.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
Every $L_1$ vertex is adjacent to $u$. Every $L_2$ vertex has an
$L_1$-neighbour (the witness counts above) and is not adjacent to $u$,
so it lies at distance~2. Every $L_3$ vertex has an $L_2$-neighbour
and all its neighbours are in $L_2$, so it lies at distance~3; $L_3
\ne \emptyset$ ($Z$ exists).
\end{proof}

\begin{claim}\label{cl:radius}
No vertex of $G(F)$ has eccentricity at most~2; hence
$\mathrm{radius}(G(F)) = 3$ exactly.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
By bipartiteness, same-part distances are even and cross-part
distances odd. (i)~A vertex $z \in L_3$ is not adjacent to $u$, so
$\mathrm{dist}(z,u) \ge 3$. (ii)~A vertex $v \in L_1$ with
$\mathrm{ecc}(v) \le 2$ would be adjacent to every vertex of the
opposite part, in particular all of $L_2$; but every $L_1$ vertex has
at most three $L_2$-neighbours while $|L_2| \ge 4$ ($a, T_1, T_2,
g_{w_a}$). (iii)~A vertex $v \in L_2$ with $\mathrm{ecc}(v) \le 2$
would be adjacent to every $L_1$ vertex; but every $L_2$ vertex has at
most two $L_1$-neighbours while $|L_1| \ge 5$ ($w_a$ and the four
ballast witnesses). (iv)~$\mathrm{ecc}(u) = 3$ by
Claim~\ref{cl:conn}.
\end{proof}

We note (machine-checked on every generated instance, though not
needed for the theorem) that $G(F)$ has diameter at least~4, so the
construction does not accidentally land in the polynomial
diameter-$\le 3$ class.

\section{The case analysis}\label{sec:cases}

Fix a matching-cut colouring $\chi$ of $G(F)$. Since the definition is
symmetric under swapping colours, assume $\chi(u) = \mathrm{R}$.

\begin{claim}\label{cl:budget}
At most one $L_1$ vertex is blue.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
$u$'s cross edges are exactly its edges to blue $L_1$ vertices.
\end{proof}

So exactly one of: \emph{Case~A} (all of $L_1$ red), split into
\emph{A1} ($\mathrm{blue} \cap L_2 \ne \emptyset$) and \emph{A0}
($\mathrm{blue} \cap L_2 = \emptyset$); or \emph{Case~B} (exactly one
$b \in L_1$ blue).

\begin{claim}\label{cl:a0}
Case A0 is impossible.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
In case A0 the blue set is contained in $L_3$ and is nonempty. A blue
$z \in L_3$ has all neighbours in $L_2$, all red, so it carries
$\deg(z)$ cross edges; but every $L_3$ vertex has degree $\ge 2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{claim}[freeze]\label{cl:freeze}
If $v \in L_2$ has two $L_1$-neighbours and both are red, then $v$ is
red.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
Blue $v$ would cross both.
\end{proof}

\begin{claim}\label{cl:caseB}
Case B is impossible.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
Let $b$ be the unique blue $L_1$ vertex. The edge $bu$ consumes $b$'s
budget, so \emph{every other neighbour of $b$ is blue}. By
Claim~\ref{cl:freeze}, every guard and ballast vertex whose two
witnesses both differ from $b$ is red. We branch on $b$; the list is
exhaustive by the $L_1$ inventory of Section~\ref{sec:construction}.

\emph{Branch 1: $b = W \in \BW$.} Then $g_W$ (a neighbour of $b$) is
blue. $g_W$'s other witness $w'_W$ is red, consuming $g_W$'s budget,
so $g_W$'s remaining neighbour $Z$ is blue. $Z$'s neighbours $T_1$
and $T_2$ are red (their witnesses are ballast witnesses, $\ne b$),
giving $Z$ two cross edges. Contradiction.

\emph{Branch 2: $b = w'_W$.} Then $g_W$ is blue, its other witness
$W$ is red and consumes its budget, $Z$ is blue, and $T_1, T_2$ are
red as before. Contradiction.

\emph{Branch 3: $b$ is a ballast witness, say of $T_1$.} Then $T_1$
is blue; its other ballast witness is red and consumes its budget, so
$Z$ is blue. $Z$'s neighbours $T_2$ (witnesses $\ne b$) and every
guard $g_W$ (both witnesses $\ne b$, red by Claim~\ref{cl:freeze})
are red; already $T_2$ and $g_{w_a}$ give $Z$ two cross edges.
Contradiction.
\end{proof}

Consequently $G(F)$ has a matching cut iff it has a \emph{case-A1}
colouring: $u$ red, $N(u)$ all red, and at least one blue $L_2$
vertex.

\section{The reformulation}\label{sec:reform}

Throughout this section $G$ is any connected bipartite graph with a
vertex $u$ of eccentricity~3 and BFS layers $L_0,\dots,L_3$. Define
$S := \{v \in L_2 : |N(v) \cap L_1| = 1\}$ and let $w : S \to L_1$
map $v$ to its unique $L_1$-neighbour.

\begin{observation}[layering]\label{obs:layer}
Every edge of $G$ joins consecutive layers: same-layer and
layer-skipping edges are excluded by bipartite parity and the BFS
structure, and there is no $L_4$ since $\mathrm{ecc}(u)=3$. In
particular $N(z) \subseteq L_2$ for $z \in L_3$, there are no
$L_2$--$L_2$ edges, and every $v \in L_2$ has at least one
$L_1$-neighbour (a BFS-layer-2 vertex has a neighbour at distance~1
from $u$).
\end{observation}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:reform}
$G$ has a matching-cut colouring with $u$ red, $N(u)$ all red, and
$\mathrm{blue} \cap L_2 \ne \emptyset$ \emph{iff} there is a nonempty
$B_2 \subseteq S$ with:
\begin{itemize}
\item[$(\alpha)$] $w$ is injective on $B_2$;
\item[$(\beta)$] every $z \in N(B_2) \cap L_3$ has
  $|N(z) \setminus B_2| \le 1$, where $N(z)$ is $z$'s full
  $L_2$-neighbourhood;
\item[$(\gamma)$] every $v \in L_2 \setminus B_2$ has
  $|N(v) \cap N(B_2) \cap L_3| \le 1$.
\end{itemize}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
($\Rightarrow$) Let $\chi$ be such a colouring and $B_2 :=
\mathrm{blue} \cap L_2 \neq \emptyset$. Every $v \in B_2$ has all its
$L_1$-neighbours red, each a cross edge; the budget and
Observation~\ref{obs:layer} force exactly one $L_1$-neighbour (so
$B_2 \subseteq S$) and no further cross, hence every $L_3$-neighbour
of $v$ is blue; so all of $N(B_2) \cap L_3$ is blue. $(\alpha)$:~a
red witness crosses each blue member of its group, and its budget is
one. $(\beta)$:~a blue $z \in L_3$ crosses exactly its red
$L_2$-neighbours, which include all of $N(z) \setminus B_2$.
$(\gamma)$:~a red $v \in L_2 \setminus B_2$ crosses each blue
$L_3$-neighbour, and $N(v) \cap N(B_2) \cap L_3$ is entirely blue.

($\Leftarrow$) Colour blue exactly $B_2 \cup (N(B_2) \cap L_3)$.
Budgets: $u$ has no blue neighbour; an $L_1$ vertex has at most one
blue neighbour by $(\alpha)$; a $v \in B_2$ crosses exactly its
witness; a $z \in N(B_2) \cap L_3$ crosses $|N(z) \setminus B_2| \le
1$ vertices by $(\beta)$; a red $v \in L_2 \setminus B_2$ crosses
$|N(v) \cap N(B_2) \cap L_3| \le 1$ by $(\gamma)$; and a red $z \in
L_3 \setminus N(B_2)$ has no blue neighbour. Both colours are used.
\end{proof}

Applying Lemma~\ref{lem:reform} to $G(F)$: the selectable set is
exactly $S = \{a\} \cup \{t_i, f_i\} \cup \{p_{C,j}\}$ (guards and
ballast have two witnesses), the group partition induced by $w$ is
$\{t_i, f_i\}$ (sharing $q_i$) plus singletons, and conditions
$(\beta),(\gamma)$ involve only the base $L_3$ vertices: $Z$'s
$L_2$-neighbours are all unselectable, so $Z \notin N(B_2)$; every
base $L_3$ vertex has an all-selectable $L_2$-neighbourhood; and each
unselectable $L_2$ vertex has $\{Z\}$ as its whole
$L_3$-neighbourhood, making $(\gamma)$ vacuous there. The
reformulated problem on $G(F)$ is therefore exactly the following set
problem on the instance $I(F)$ built from the formula.

\section{The set problem and the equivalence}\label{sec:island}

\textsc{Island}: given a bipartite graph $(X, \mathcal{Z})$ and a
partition of $X$ into groups, decide whether some nonempty $B
\subseteq X$ with at most one member per group satisfies: every $z
\in N(B)$ has at most one neighbour outside $B$, and every $x \in X
\setminus B$ has at most one neighbour in $N(B)$.

The instance $I(F)$: $X = \{a\} \cup \{t_i, f_i\} \cup \{p_{C,j}\}$
with groups $\{t_i,f_i\}$ and singletons; $\mathcal{Z}$-side:
$\gamma_i \sim (a, t_i, f_i)$; $c_{C,j,k} \sim (\mathrm{base}(C,j),
p_{C,j})$ for $k \in \{0,1\}$; $z_C \sim (p_{C,1}, p_{C,2},
p_{C,3})$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:island}
$\textsc{Island}(I(F))$ is a YES-instance iff $F$ is satisfiable.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
($\Leftarrow$) Given a satisfying assignment $\sigma$, take $B :=
\{a\} \cup \{t_i : \sigma(x_i) = 1\} \cup \{f_i : \sigma(x_i) = 0\}
\cup \{p_{C,j} : \text{the } (C,j) \text{ literal is true}\}$. The
group condition holds by construction. Every gate $\gamma_i$ is
touched and has exactly one of $t_i, f_i$ outside. A copy is touched
iff its base is in $B$ iff its proxy is in $B$, so touched copies
have no neighbour outside. A clause vertex $z_C$ is touched iff its
true-literal count $t_C \ge 1$, and then has $3 - t_C \le 1$ outside
since $t_C \in \{0,2,3\}$. For the outside condition: an unselected
literal vertex sees only its gate among touched $\mathcal{Z}$
vertices; an unselected proxy sees at most $z_C$.

($\Rightarrow$) Let $B$ be any solution. (1)~A selected proxy pulls
its base: $p_{C,j} \in B$ touches both copies; if the base were
outside it would have two touched neighbours. (2)~A selected base
pulls $a$: $t_i \in B$ touches $\gamma_i$, whose neighbour $f_i$ is
group-blocked, so $a \in B$ (else two outside). (3)~Nonempty $\Rightarrow
a \in B$, by (1) and (2). (4)~$a \in B$ forces a full assignment:
every gate is touched, so at least one of $t_i, f_i$ is in $B$, and
the group caps it at exactly one; set $\sigma(x_i) := 1$ iff $t_i \in
B$. (5)~A selected base pulls its proxies (two touched copies at an
outside proxy), so with (1): $p_{C,j} \in B \iff \mathrm{base}(C,j)
\in B \iff$ the $(C,j)$ literal is true under $\sigma$. (6)~A touched
$z_C$ has at most one proxy outside, so $t_C \ge 2$; an untouched
$z_C$ has $t_C = 0$. Either way $t_C \ne 1$: $\sigma$ satisfies $F$.
Repeated literals are unproblematic since proxies and copies are
per-position.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
$G(F)$ is constructible in linear time, bipartite
(Claim~\ref{cl:bip}), and of radius exactly~3
(Claims~\ref{cl:conn},~\ref{cl:radius}). It has a matching cut iff a
case-A1 colouring exists (Section~\ref{sec:cases}), iff
$\textsc{Island}(I(F))$ is YES (Lemma~\ref{lem:reform} and the
discussion following it), iff $F$ is satisfiable
(Lemma~\ref{lem:island}). The source problem is NP-complete
(Section~\ref{sec:source}), and membership is trivial.
\end{proof}

\section{Provenance and verification}\label{sec:provenance}

This result was produced by an AI-driven research process directed by
the author. The construction, proofs, and repairs are the work of
Claude (Anthropic); the base construction (the unguarded core of
Section~\ref{sec:construction}) originated with GPT-5.6 (OpenAI) in a
cross-model consultation, whose original graph-level reduction was
\emph{refuted} by the machine testing described below before being
repaired with the guard battery.

The verification regime, in order of application:
(1)~\emph{machine testing}: the construction and every lemma-level
claim implemented as code and checked end-to-end against
independently implemented exact oracles on thousands of instances,
including exhaustive slices and unsatisfiable-focused batteries, with
mutation controls confirming the test harness detects deliberately
broken variants; (2)~\emph{adversarial model review}: four reviews
across three model families (two fresh-context Claude instances,
Gemini~3.1~Pro, Kimi~K3), each instructed to refute; the first review
found a genuine scoping error in an earlier form of
Lemma~\ref{lem:reform}, which was repaired and re-attacked;
(3)~\emph{formalization}: the combinatorial core --- the construction,
Claims~\ref{cl:bip}--\ref{cl:radius}, and the equivalence of
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} in both directions --- is proven in Lean~4
against mathlib with zero \texttt{sorry}s (axioms:
\texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice}, \texttt{Quot.sound}
only), including a non-vacuousness witness: an explicitly
unsatisfiable formula whose graph is kernel-proven to admit no
matching cut. The NP-hardness of the source problem and the
polynomial-time bound of the reduction are standard and are not
formalized. All code, verification logs, review transcripts, and the
Lean development are public in the accompanying repository.

\section{Open problems}

The sibling cell of~\cite{LMPS25} remains open:
\textsc{Disconnected Perfect Matching} on bipartite graphs of
radius~3. We have not examined whether the present construction is
compatible with the perfect-matching requirement.
